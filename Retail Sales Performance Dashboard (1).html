Analytical summary (full dataset)

Key findingEvidenceBusiness implication
Sales grew every month.₱370,000 in January to ₱510,000 in April, up 37.8%. Monthly gains: +14.9%, +10.6%, +8.5%.Demand is rising, but the rate of growth is slowing each month.
Laptops drive nearly half of revenue.₱815,000 of ₱1,775,000 (45.9%) from only 82 units. Mobile phones: ₱665,000 (37.5%). Tablets: ₱295,000 (16.6%).Revenue depends heavily on high-priced laptops. Stock and pricing there matter most.
Mobile phones lead in volume.220 of 400 units (55%), but average price per unit is about ₱3,023 versus about ₱9,939 for laptops.Phones bring customers in. Laptops bring the revenue.
North leads, but regions are close.North ₱475,000, South ₱450,000, West ₱430,000, East ₱420,000. The gap from first to last is only ₱55,000 (11.6% of North).No region is failing. Small gains in East and West would narrow the gap.
Tablets are the weakest line.₱295,000 total (16.6% of revenue) from 98 units, and tablets appear in only 4 of 16 records.Tablets add little revenue. Management should decide whether to promote them or reduce stock.

Recommendations

  • Study April. It was the best month (₱510,000), led by North laptops (₱160,000). Find what drove it and repeat it in other regions.
  • Protect laptop supply and bundle phones with accessories. Laptops earn the most per sale; phones sell the most units.
  • Review tablet strategy. Run a targeted promotion in East and West, or reduce tablet stock if demand stays low.
  • Share North's practices with East and West to close the regional gap.

Note: each row in the dataset is one product in one region and month, so not every product appears in every region. Filtered totals reflect only those rows.
